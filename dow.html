Understanding Zeller's Congruence

Zeller's congruence is an algorithm devised by Christian Zeller in 1882 to calculate the day of the week for any Julian or Gregorian calendar date.

$$h = \left( q + \lfloor \frac{13(m+1)}{5} \rfloor + K + \lfloor \frac{K}{4} \rfloor + \lfloor \frac{J}{4} \rfloor - 2J \right) \pmod 7$$

h is the day of the week (0=Sat, 1=Sun...)

q is the day of the month

m is the month (Mar=3, Apr=4, ..., Jan=13, Feb=14)

K is the year of the century (year mod 100)

J is the zero-based century (floor of year/100)

*Note: Jan & Feb are counted as months 13 & 14 of the previous year.

Calculations follow the Gregorian calendar rules.
